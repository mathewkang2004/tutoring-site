Unit 1: Close Reading & Literary Analysis

Key Concepts

Theme vs. topic

A topic is what a work is about in one or two words (loss, ambition, family). A theme is the insight or claim the work makes about that topic — a full sentence. “Ambition” is a topic; “unchecked ambition corrodes relationships” is a theme.

Tone and mood

  • Tone = the author’s/narrator’s attitude toward the subject (sarcastic, reverent, bitter).
  • Mood = the emotional atmosphere the reader feels (tense, nostalgic, eerie).

These are shaped by diction (word choice), syntax (sentence structure), and imagery.

Literary devices worth naming precisely

Device What it does
Symbolism An object/image represents a larger idea
Foreshadowing Hints at events to come
Irony Gap between expectation and reality (situational, verbal, dramatic)
Motif A recurring element that reinforces a theme
Characterization How a character is revealed (direct statement vs. actions/dialogue = indirect)

Writing literary analysis

The core move is: claim → evidence → analysis. Don’t just quote the text — explain how the quote’s specific word choice or structure supports your claim. A weak analysis restates the quote; a strong one explains the effect of the technique.

TipUpgrade weak analysis

Instead of “This shows the author uses imagery,” try: “The imagery of ‘rust creeping along the fence’ mirrors the slow decay of the family’s relationship, making the abstract idea of estrangement feel physically inevitable.”

Practice Problems

  1. Turn this topic into a theme statement: “isolation” (as explored in a story where a character withdraws after a loss).
NoteSolution

Sample theme: “Isolation, while initially protective, ultimately prevents healing by cutting a person off from the support they need.” (Any full-sentence claim that goes beyond the single word works.)

  1. A character is described by the narrator as “honest and kind,” but the story shows them lying to protect their own reputation. What’s the difference between the direct and indirect characterization here, and what might it suggest?
NoteSolution

The narrator’s description is direct characterization (“honest and kind” stated outright). The character’s actions (lying) are indirect characterization. The contradiction suggests unreliable narration or that the character’s self-image doesn’t match reality — a gap worth analyzing.

  1. Identify the device: a storm builds throughout a chapter, then breaks into violence right as two characters have their final argument.
NoteSolution

This is symbolism (the storm represents the emotional conflict) and could also be discussed as foreshadowing if earlier hints pointed to the coming argument.
